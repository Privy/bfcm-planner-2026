**Scope:** these practices cover prep through the Friday after Cyber Monday. Holiday gifting and December programs are planned separately.

Working-back milestones (adapted from Privy's BFCM calendar, which set offer lock in late September, send schedule lock in early October, and a final list-growth push about three weeks before Black Friday):

## 2. What the market looks like

- Shopify merchants did a record $14.6B over BFCM 2025, up 27% YoY; average cart was $114.70, Shop Pay handled 32% of orders, and cross-border orders were 16% of the total. Sales peaked at 12:01 pm ET on Black Friday. (Shopify)
- Adobe's 2025 Cyber Week (Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday) was $44.2B online, up 7.7%; Black Friday hit $11.8B and Cyber Monday $14.25B, with 57.5% of Cyber Monday sales on mobile. Adobe noted persistent deals pushed shoppers to buy earlier, so Black Friday now challenges Cyber Monday. (Adobe, Dec 2025)
- Adobe's 2026 forecast (published Sep 2026): $275.1B US online holiday sales (+6.7%); Cyber Week $47.5B (+7.4%), about 17% of the season; a record 63% of Cyber Week spend on mobile; discounts peaking around 30% off during Cyber Week, up to 14% in early November and up to 21% just before Thanksgiving. (Adobe)
- Discounts and flexible payments pulled spending earlier and spread it across the season rather than concentrating it in one week (Adobe). Plan for early-November demand, not just Black Friday.

**Implications:** the season starts earlier than Black Friday, mobile is the default experience, and at this revenue band the brands that win lean on their own email and SMS audiences and existing customers rather than just deeper discounts.

## 3. What wins at this revenue band

Brands at $1M-$20M rarely win a discount-depth war against big-box retailers. They win by:
1. **Owning the audience before the sale** (list growth plus early access).
2. **Rewarding existing customers first** (VIP early access, thank-you tone, gifts rather than deeper discounts).
3. **Protecting margin with offer structure** (tiers, bundles, gift with purchase) rather than one deep sitewide cut.
4. **Operational reliability** (every code, flow, popup, and landing page works; no stacking accidents; no sold-out products being promoted).
5. **Converting deal buyers into repeat customers** after the sale.

## 4. Offer architecture

Start from economics, not from what competitors are doing. Ask for or estimate: gross margin (overall and by hero products), fulfillment cost per order, typical return rate, and the maximum discount the business can afford by product or bundle. Then choose the structure.

| Structure | Best for | Margin impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tiered spend (e.g., spend $100 save 15%, $150 save 20%, $200 save 25%) | Broad catalogs, mid AOV | Medium | Lifts AOV; keep tiers dead simple and anchored to current AOV (first tier roughly 1.2-1.5x AOV). |
| Gift with purchase over a threshold | Premium, beauty, brands protecting price | Low (cost of gift) | Preserves price perception; good for introducing a new product. |
| Bundles / kits at a "valued at" price | Complementary products, gifting | Low-medium | Name the bundle for the job it does or the recipient. |
| BOGO / buy 2 get 1 | High-repeat consumables | Medium | Drives stock-up; pair with replenishment flow. |
| Flat sitewide % off | Commodity categories, clearance | High | Simple and effective for volume; biggest margin and conditioning risk. |
| Free shipping threshold | Low-AOV brands | Low-medium | Set just above current AOV. |

Guidelines:
- **One headline offer, clearly stated.** Complexity kills conversion. A layered offer (modest sitewide + GWP over a threshold) is fine if it can be said in one line.
- **Margin tiers:** discount or bundle high-margin items; keep thin-margin items at full price or use them only as add-ons.
- **Kill stacking.** Audit every live code: welcome, popup, spin-to-win, SMS signup, cart recovery, birthday, win-back, influencer, and loyalty. Decide per code: pause, replace with the BFCM offer, or allow. Use Shopify discount combination settings deliberately.
- **Plan the second act.** Decide in advance whether Cyber Monday is the same offer or a new one, and whether you extend through Friday. Do not change discount logic mid-sale unless something is broken.
- **Early access is an offer.** A 24-48 hour head start for VIPs and signups is valuable without any extra discount.
- **Use Adobe's discount curve as context** (up to ~14% early November, ~21% pre-Thanksgiving, ~30% Cyber Week) rather than a target; the merchant's margin decides.

## 5. Calendar and cadence

Suggested core campaign spine (from Privy's readiness checklist and broader practice):

| When | Send | Audience | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5-7 days before launch | Teaser (email + SMS) | Engaged list | Hint the offer, push early-access signup, give exact launch date and time. |
| 12-48 hours before public launch | VIP / Early access (SMS first, then email) | VIPs + early-access signups | Unique link or code; suppress those who buy from the public launch send. |
| Launch moment | Sale is live (SMS first) | Everyone eligible | Confirm site banner, popups, and codes are live first. |
| Mid-sale (Sat/Sun) | New angle: bestsellers, bundles, what's selling | Non-purchasers | Exclude buyers. |
| Cyber Monday morning | Cyber Monday push | Non-purchasers | Own subject lines and creative if it is a distinct offer. |
| 3-6 hours before the real end | Last chance (email), final SMS | Non-purchasers, engaged | Only before the actual end of the offer. If extending, this send moves to Fri Dec 4. SMS inside quiet hours. |
| Tue Dec 1 morning | Extension announcement (only if planned) | Non-purchasers, engaged | Say plainly what is extended and until when. |
| Wed-Thu Dec 2-3 | One mid-extension send, new angle | Non-purchasers, engaged | Lighter cadence than the weekend. |
| Fri Dec 4, final hours | Extension last chance | Non-purchasers | Real end time; SMS inside quiet hours. |

**Extension window (Tue-Fri after Cyber Monday):** worth running when the weekend still has unconverted demand. Decide it before launch, because it changes what Monday's sends are allowed to say (section 11a). If the same deal continues, Monday's emails, SMS, and timers cannot say it ends Monday. Say "Cyber Monday deals end tonight" only if what ends is really different (a Monday-only offer or products) from what continues. Give it a firm Friday end time, send less than the weekend (roughly one email a day to engaged contacts and at most two SMS over the four days), keep excluding purchasers, and watch spam complaints closely since fatigue peaks here. If you don't extend, end cleanly Monday night and move straight to wrap-up.

Cadence rules of thumb for this band: engaged email subscribers can take 1-2 sends per day during the peak days; send less to less-engaged tiers and nothing high-frequency to the unengaged segment. SMS: plan roughly 4-6 promotional SMS across the whole window for engaged subscribers, and respect the state frequency caps in section 10. Always exclude recent purchasers from discount sends.

**Don't cut volume because engagement falls across the sale.** Click rates always drop and unsubscribes always rise as BFCM goes on; that's expected, not a reason to send less. Judge each send by what it brought in against what it cost: attributed revenue vs. unsubscribes times the contact's yearly value (Privy-attributed revenue per contact per year). If even the weakest sends roughly break even or better, keep the volume. Remember that last-touch attribution overstates what a single mid-sale email adds, so look hardest at the tail sends. The usual real fix is who gets the sends, not how many: leave unengaged contacts out of every sale send, exclude recent purchasers from discount sends, and stop sending to anyone beyond engaged contacts if spam complaints pass 0.1% on a send.

Test in October, not November: subject lines, offer framing, send times. Lock winners before launch.

**SMS is for high intent.** Use SMS in cart and checkout abandonment, back in stock, early access, and the welcome step after an SMS signup. Never add SMS to browse abandonment: it's too far up the funnel. Leave an existing browse SMS step alone.

**Send timing in Privy.** Privy holds any SMS that would land in a contact's quiet hours until quiet hours end, and SMS and emails can be sent by each contact's time zone. Use timezone-based sending only for sends that aren't tied to the sale going live: teasers and regular emails before the sale. Launch, sale-day, and deadline sends go out on the store's clock, so every shopper hears the sale is live, or ending, at the same moment. A 9am Eastern launch sent by time zone reaches Pacific shoppers three hours late. Schedule a few minutes off the hour or half hour (9:07, not 9:00) to avoid the queue at the top of the hour.

**October list work.** Before the first sale send: re-engage then suppress contacts who haven't opened in 90-180 days, grow the SMS list with a consented SMS step on the popup, and send at least one normal email to the audience you'll mail at BFCM, so the first big send isn't a cold spike. A merchant returning from a quiet stretch needs this as a warm-up (see new-to-privy.md).

## 6. List growth and on-site (Privy displays)

List growth is the highest-leverage pre-BFCM activity, and it compounds: every subscriber added in October is reachable at the peak.

- **Early-access capture** (launch 2-3 weeks before BF at the latest; earlier is better): homepage and top collections, promise a specific head start ("24 hours early"), connect to the Early Access Signup flow (F03). Use a countdown only to the real signup or launch deadline.
- **Before the sale, trade discounts for access.** Exit-intent and welcome popups can offer early access instead of a code, which avoids training visitors to wait and prevents stacking.
- **During the sale, switch popups to the live deal** and show returning subscribers the deal or a cart reminder rather than another signup ask.
- **Spin-to-win:** make sure no prize beats or stacks with the sitewide offer; consider free shipping or a gift instead.
- **Timing:** show signup displays sooner before the sale (5-10 seconds), then limit to once per session during it so deal shoppers are not slowed down.
- **Mobile first:** 63% of Cyber Week spend is forecast on mobile. Use slim bars or flyouts on mobile; never cover the add-to-cart or checkout button; check display script weight.
- **SMS capture:** a two-step form (email then phone) and a post-purchase thank-you page SMS opt-in ("fastest way to hear about restocks and Cyber Monday drops") grow SMS without extra discounting.
- **Social and existing email subscribers:** promote early access on social and invite email-only subscribers to join SMS.

## 7. Segmentation

Choose from the 20 segments in `checklist-segments.md` using `selection-rules.md`; most stores need 6-14. Set thresholds from the merchant's data, not generic numbers:
- **VIP threshold:** roughly the top 5-10% of customers by lifetime spend, or 3+ orders. State the actual dollar value and resulting segment size.
- **High-spend cutoff** for the spend/frequency matrix: roughly the top 20-25% by lifetime spend.
- **Engagement tiers** for cadence: engaged 30 days, 60 days, 90 days, and unengaged 90+ (suppressed from high frequency).
- **Suppression is a segment:** Recent Buyers 7D (launch email only), unengaged, bounced, unsubscribed, and unshippable regions.

## 8. Flows

Choose from the 15 flows in `checklist-flows.md` using `selection-rules.md`; most stores need 6-10. Highest leverage during BFCM, in order: Abandoned Cart (shorten first delay to 30-60 min, remove standalone discount during sitewide sale, add SMS), Welcome (swap to BFCM teaser or live offer, shorten delays), Early Access Signup (net-new), VIP entry, Back in Stock, Order Confirmation (shipping cutoffs, suppress discount CTAs 14 days). Pause or quiet: Win-Back, Birthday, Review Request during BFCM week. Build now, launch after Cyber Monday: Post-BFCM New Customer Nurture.

Log every flow change in a revert log with owner and revert date.

## 9. Deliverability (email)

Mailbox provider rules are enforced, not optional, and BFCM volume spikes are when reputations break.
- Gmail and Yahoo require bulk senders (5,000+ messages a day to their users) to authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC (p=none at minimum, with alignment), support one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) on marketing mail and honor it within about two days, and keep spam complaint rates below 0.3%. Aim for under 0.1%. Google escalated enforcement to permanent rejections in November 2025. Microsoft (Outlook, Hotmail, Live) began enforcing authentication requirements for high-volume senders in May 2025.
- **Warm up:** ramp volume to engaged segments through October and early November so the BFCM spike is not a sudden jump.
- **Re-engage then sunset:** run a re-permission send in October ("Still want our Black Friday deals?"); exclude non-responders who have been inactive 90-180+ days from high-frequency sends.
- **Monitor daily during BFCM:** Google Postmaster Tools spam rate, bounces, unsubscribes. Pull volume back if complaints rise.
- **Preference center:** offer a "pause until the sale ends" option as an alternative to unsubscribing.
- **Seed test** key templates before the first big send; watch image-to-text balance and link count.

## 10. SMS compliance and cadence

Present as conservative best practice; recommend the merchant confirm with counsel.
- **Consent:** only SMS subscribers with documented prior express written consent. Audit collection sources before increasing frequency.
- **Quiet hours:** federal guidance is 8am-9pm recipient local time. Several states set stricter windows; Florida and Oklahoma limit solicitation SMS to 8am-8pm and cap messages at three per 24 hours on the same subject, and other states (sources variously list Washington, Maryland, Connecticut, Texas, Oregon) have their own windows or caps. Sources disagree on some states, which is itself the reason to be conservative. **Recommended default: send promotional SMS between 9am and 8pm in the recipient's local time, and no more than three BFCM SMS to any subscriber in a rolling 24 hours.**
- **Final-hour SMS** must still land inside that window in every time zone; schedule by recipient time zone.
- **Identify the brand** in every promotional SMS. Privy adds the opt-out language automatically, so don't write "Reply STOP to opt out" into the copy, or it appears twice. Opt-outs are honored immediately.
- **Order:** SMS first for time-sensitive moments (early access, launch, back in stock, last chance), email for storytelling and product depth.

## 11. Site, tech, and ops

- Place test orders with every BFCM code and combination; confirm stacking behaves as intended.
- Confirm inventory sync so flows and product blocks do not promote sold-out items; pick fallback products for likely sellouts. Check weeks-of-cover on hero SKUs against last year's BFCM velocity.
- Page speed on mobile: remove unused apps and scripts, compress sale banners.
- Build a BFCM landing page or sale hub and point every campaign, popup, and banner to it; QA on mobile before the teaser.
- UTM every link; verify pixels on sale pages and checkout.
- Accurate promotional claims: see section 11a.
- Shop Pay and BNPL options matter at peak (Shop Pay was 32% of Shopify BFCM orders; Adobe expects BNPL spend to peak on Cyber Monday).

## 11a. Urgency and promotional claims

Deadline and scarcity words are factual claims, not tone. Present this as guidance and recommend counsel review.

**Why it matters now:**
- In *Brown v. Old Navy* (April 2025), the Washington Supreme Court held that the state's Commercial Electronic Mail Act prohibits any false or misleading information in a commercial email subject line, including the duration or availability of a promotion. The claims involved emails announcing promotions would end on a date, when the promotions continued. Nearly 200 class actions followed. Washington amended the law effective June 11, 2026 (HB 2274): damages dropped to $100 per email and a knowledge requirement was added. The exposure is smaller, not gone, and a pre-planned extension behind a "last chance" subject line is exactly the kind of thing a sender knows.
- The FTC's 2022 dark-patterns report names baseless countdown timers (that reset or disappear while the offer continues) and false limited-time messages as deceptive design, enforceable under Section 5 of the FTC Act.
- Other states' consumer-protection laws and other countries' rules also apply to merchants selling there.

**Rules for every send, display, and landing page:**
1. **Decide the end before writing "ends."** Lock the extension decision (and the real end date and time, with time zone) before any deadline copy is drafted.
2. **"Last chance," "final hours," "ends tonight," and "last day"** only in the final send before the offer actually ends. If the same deal continues into the extension, Monday is not the last chance.
3. **If extending, say so plainly.** Announce the extension as what it is ("We're keeping the sale going through Friday"). Don't retroactively extend an offer you told people was over. Deciding on the fly Tuesday morning after "ends tonight" sends is the pattern that got sued.
4. **Countdown timers** count down to the real end, don't reset per visitor or session, and come down (or switch to the new real end) when the offer ends.
5. **Scarcity claims** ("almost gone," "only 12 left," "selling fast") only when backed by live inventory at send time.
6. **Discount claims** ("up to 40% off," "save $50") must match real prices, and "up to" needs a meaningful share of items at that level. Exclusions are stated clearly.
7. **Subject lines and preview text get the strictest check,** since that's where the Washington cases focused.

**Honest urgency that works:** a real end time stated precisely ("Ends Monday at 11:59pm PT"); early access that really is early; real low stock on hero items; a Monday-only offer that really is Monday-only.

**Pre-send claims check (add to the plan as a P1 item):** for each scheduled send, confirm the subject line, preview text, body, and any timer match the real offer and end time as of that send's date, including whether an extension follows.

## 12. Measurement

Set goals before launch:
- **No forecasts.** Show last year's BFCM window revenue from Shopify as a baseline (an observation, not a target). Record the merchant's own goal only if they volunteer one, labeled as theirs. Don't propose revenue numbers or growth rates.
- **Attribution:** Shopify counts revenue on the order date; Privy attributes it to the send date. Compare Shopify to Shopify day by day, read Privy per send, and use window totals when combining them.
- **Goals the merchant controls:** early-access and SMS signups by Nov 20, P1 flows live by a date, every code tested, spam complaints under 0.1%.
- **Owned-channel share:** email and SMS attributed revenue as a share of BFCM revenue, compared with the merchant's own history in Privy.
- **Watch live:** revenue pace by hour, conversion rate, AOV vs tier thresholds, popup signup rate, flow revenue, spam complaints, unsubscribes, inventory on hero SKUs.
- **Window close (Sat Dec 5):** revenue vs last year for core and extension (and vs the merchant's own goal, if they set one), contribution margin after discounts and shipping, new vs returning mix, discount rate, list growth. Schedule the full recap 1-2 weeks later, once returns settle, and tag BFCM-acquired customers so their repeat rate can be tracked afterward.

## 13. Wrap-up and handoff (Sat Dec 5)

BFCM is an acquisition event, so the plan ends by setting up what comes next rather than planning December.
- Revert every BFCM change in the revert log (restored delays, codes, paused flows, suppressions).
- Turn on the Post-BFCM New Customer Nurture (F12), built before the sale, timed to start after delivery.
- Tag or segment the BFCM-acquired cohort so its second-purchase rate can be tracked.
- Schedule the recap once returns settle; note what to repeat and what to cut.
- Hand off to holiday planning: gifting, shipping-cutoff campaigns, and December offers are a separate plan.

## 14. Tailoring by GMV band

| | $1M-$5M | $5M-$20M |
|---|---|---|
| Typical team | Owner plus one marketer, or freelancer | Dedicated retention marketer and/or agency, some dev support |
| Offer | One simple headline offer (plus early access) | Phased offers (early access, BF, CM), tiers or GWP, testing in October |
| Segments | 6-8 (core plus suppressions) | 10-14, adding category, gift, or spend-matrix segments only where messages differ |
| Flows | Core flows plus adjustments to existing ones (typically 6-8) | Core plus conditional flows that apply (typically 8-12) |
| Campaigns | Core 4-6 sends per channel | Full calendar with engagement-tiered cadence |
| Reporting | Daily revenue vs last year and complaint check | Live dashboard owner, daily snapshot, formal recap |
